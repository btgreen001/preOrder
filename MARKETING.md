1. Go straight to where bakers already talk (high‑impact, zero cost)
Facebook Groups (the #1 channel for bakers)
Independent bakeries live in:

“Bakery Business” groups

“Cottage Food Operators” groups

“Cake Decorators” groups

Local small‑business groups

These groups are extremely active and full of people complaining about:

Thanksgiving pie chaos

Pickup scheduling

Overbooking

Google Forms disasters

How to post without being salesy
Instead of pitching, post something like:

“I built a tiny tool called BakeAhead to help bakeries manage holiday pre‑orders without spreadsheets. I’m looking for 3–5 bakeries to try it free for their next holiday rush. If you’ve ever had pickup‑day chaos, I’d love your feedback.”

This works because:

It’s helpful, not salesy

Bakers love being early testers

You get real users fast

2. Use Instagram — but not like a marketer
Bakeries are visual businesses. They’re on Instagram constantly.

What to post
Short reels showing how easy it is to set up a holiday menu

Before/after examples of chaotic spreadsheets vs. BakeAhead

A mock “Thanksgiving Pre‑Order” flow

Who to tag
Local bakeries

Cottage bakers

Baking educators

Local food bloggers

DM strategy (non‑spammy)
Send a message like:

“Hey! I built a simple pre‑order tool for bakeries to avoid holiday chaos. If you ever do Thanksgiving or Christmas pre‑orders, I’d love to give you a free setup to try it.”

This feels like a favor, not a pitch.

3. Partner with cottage bakers (they’re your early adopters)
Cottage bakers:

Are solo operators

Hate admin work

Do tons of holiday pre‑orders

Are extremely vocal online

Offer them:

Free use for one holiday

A testimonial in exchange

They’ll talk about it publicly if it helps them.

Make a simple PDF or blog post:

“How to Avoid Thanksgiving Pickup Chaos”

“The 7 Mistakes Bakeries Make With Holiday Pre‑Orders”

Share it in groups, forums, and Instagram.

Inside it, mention:

“This guide was created by BakeAhead — a simple tool for managing holiday pre‑orders.”

This positions you as a helpful expert.

Bakers are busy. If you say:
